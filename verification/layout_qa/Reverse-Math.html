Bottom-Up Reverse Math

P = Democratic primary-ballot share (proxy — Arkansas has no party registration). 2026 general-election figures are UNOFFICIAL (not yet certified statewide; Arkansas SOS results feed v234, Nov 19 2026). Governor race only. These pages measure a pattern; they make no claim about intent, fraud or cause.

The same arithmetic at three levels: one county, then the six largest, then the whole state. It is then solved backward for implied extra votes. S = Democratic governor votes ÷ total ballots cast. The method comes from the Cedar County Audit in Nebraska.

Step 1: Dallas County (the ceiling county)

Step Value
Democratic share of ballots (S) 34.76% (2022: 732 of 2,106) → 38.69% (2026: 674 of 1,742): +3.93 points
Proxy P [Democratic primary-ballot share (proxy — Arkansas has no party registration)] 44.96% (557 of 1,239) → 9.36% (123 of 1,314): −35.59 points
ΔG = ΔS − ΔP 3.93 − (−35.59) = +39.53 points, the largest in Arkansas
Reverse step: ΔG − proxy-share loss 39.53 − 35.59 = +3.93 (the residual)
Implied extra votes 674 − 34.76% × 1,742 = about +69
Primary-scaled gap (Nebraska "gap in votes", for comparison) 674 − 732 × (123 ÷ 557) = about +512

Dallas's ΔG is large because its proxy share collapsed: Democratic Senate-primary votes fell from 557 to 123 while total primary ballots went from 1,239 to 1,314. Its residual, +3.93, is close to the state's typical ΔS (median +2.17).

Solving backward: implied extra votes (hypothesis)

For each county, implied extra votes = 2026 Democratic votes − (2022 Democratic share of ballots × 2026 ballots). The 75 counties sum to +23,537; on pooled statewide numbers it is +30,353. Largest: Benton (+5,518), Washington (+2,944), Saline (+1,604). Smallest: Franklin (−464), Logan (−203), Conway (−81).

Hypothesis label. Turning the residual into "implied extra votes" and calling them injected, switched or offset votes is a hypothesis the arithmetic cannot test. The same number reflects candidate appeal, turnout and crossover voting.

What the arithmetic cannot tell you

  • Who cast the ballots, or why S moved.
  • Whether a primary-ballot shift reflects party loyalty or one contested local race. Dallas's collapse in Democratic Senate-primary votes is exactly the kind of move a local race can cause.
  • Anything certified about 2026: the general-election figures are UNOFFICIAL.
