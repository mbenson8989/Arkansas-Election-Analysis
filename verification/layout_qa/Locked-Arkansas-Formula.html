Fitted by least squares across all 75 counties (n = 75): 2022 to 2026 governor race, ballots-cast denominator, locked gap formula. Proxy: Arkansas has no party registration, so the registration share is replaced by P = Democratic primary-ballot share (proxy — Arkansas has no party registration): Democratic U.S. Senate primary votes ÷ total primary ballots cast. Primary-proxy drop = P 2022 − P 2026, in points (negative where the proxy rose). 2026 general-election figures are UNOFFICIAL; this line should be re-fitted after certification.

What it means: across Arkansas's counties, each 1-point drop in the Democratic primary-proxy share goes with about 0.98 points of ΔG, starting from about +2.09 when the proxy does not change. Where the proxy rose, the drop is negative and ΔG falls by about the same amount. The fit explains about 90% of the county-to-county differences in ΔG. This line describes the county pattern; it does not prove anything.

Worked example: Pulaski (largest county). P went from 45.28 to 64.09, a primary-proxy drop of −18.8099 points (the proxy rose). Predicted ΔG = +2.0947 + 0.9792 × (−18.8099) = −16.32. Actual ΔG = −18.14. Difference from the fitted line (actual − predicted) = −1.82. This difference is not the line-11 residual in the template below, which is ΔG minus the proxy-share loss and applies only to counties that lost Democratic Senate-primary votes.

The equation

  • S (Democratic vote share) = Democratic governor votes ÷ total ballots cast × 100. Ballots cast is the SOS county turnout figure.
  • P (Democratic primary-ballot share (proxy — Arkansas has no party registration)) = Democratic U.S. Senate primary votes ÷ total primary ballots cast × 100, from the same year's preferential primary (May 24, 2022; March 3, 2026).
  • G = S − P
  • ΔG = G(2026) − G(2022) = (S2026 − S2022) − (P2026 − P2022)
  • Reverse step (residual). Run it only where Democratic Senate-primary votes fell. Residual = ΔG − |P2026 − P2022|.
  • Solved backward: implied extra votes = D2026 − (S2022 ÷ 100) × ballots2026
  • Primary-scaled gap (the Nebraska wiki's "gap in votes" line, for comparison) = D2026 − D2022 × (DemPrimary2026 ÷ DemPrimary2022)

Why this proxy

  • Arkansas has no meaningful party registration to count: party choice on the registration form is optional, and the SOS publishes no registration totals by party.
  • The SOS files give one ballots-cast figure per county for the whole primary (Democratic, Republican and nonpartisan judicial ballots together). They do not give ballots by party. The denominator is that total, so P is the Democratic share of all primary ballots.
  • The numerator needs a Democratic contest that is statewide and contested in both years. The governor primary fails: the 2026 Republican governor primary was not on the ballot (Sanders was unopposed), and the SOS feed's 2022 Democratic governor primary is missing almost all of James "Rus" Russell's 6,421 votes. The Democratic U.S. Senate primary was contested statewide in both years (2022: James, Whitfield, Foster; 2026: Shoffner, Dunbar), and its statewide totals in the feed match the published results exactly (91,932 and 130,683).
  • Senate-primary votes undercount Democratic ballots by the Democratic voters who skipped that race.
  • Sensitivity: using the two-party Senate-primary share (Dem ÷ (Dem + Rep), South Carolina style) instead gives county ΔG values that correlate 0.992 with the main ones.

The locked Arkansas constants are the all-county fits: 2022 m = −42.03, b = +17.57; 2026 m = −41.03, b = +17.63. The 2026 constant is built on UNOFFICIAL general-election figures and should be re-locked after certification. Narrowed fits leave out Chicot, Dallas, Desha, Franklin, Jefferson, Lee, Phillips, Pulaski, Sevier, Washington (see Floor and Ceiling Counties).

  • The 2022 and 2026 lines are almost the same. In a pooled model with year terms, the slope changes by +1.00 (p = 0.83) and the intercept by +0.06 (p = 0.96).
  • What m means. G already contains −P, so m = −100 would mean S does not move with P at all. Arkansas's m of −42.03 (2022) and −41.03 (2026) means S rises roughly 0.58 to 0.59 points for each point of proxy share. It is not an "injection multiplier."
  • Reverse-factoring constant across counties. Regressing ΔG on ΔP gives ΔG = −0.979 × ΔP + 2.09 (SE of intercept 0.33; R² 0.902; residual SE 2.66). A slope close to −1 means ΔG moves almost point for point, in the opposite direction, with ΔP. The intercept, +2.09 points, is the typical county rise in S once the proxy change is removed.

Rules for using this formula

  1. Always label P as a proxy. Arkansas has no party registration. Every table and chart that uses P must say so.
  2. If a county lost Democratic Senate-primary voters, run the residual step. In Arkansas that is 23 counties. The other 52 are non-loss counties and get raw numbers only.
  3. The starting point is different in every state. Arkansas's constants come from Arkansas's own counties. Its P is a primary share, so its G values aren't comparable with states that have real party registration.
  4. Always pull out the ceiling and floor counties. In Arkansas the ceiling county is Dallas (+39.53), and the lowest ΔG is Pulaski (−18.14). The floor (most Republican baseline) is Searcy, Newton, Cross, Prairie, Montgomery. See Floor and Ceiling Counties.
  5. Re-run after certification. The 2026 general figures are UNOFFICIAL.

Terms used in this wiki (full definitions on Floor and Ceiling Counties):

  • Ceiling counties: the five largest ΔG (Dallas, Sevier, St. Francis, Calhoun, Nevada).
  • Floor counties: the most Republican baseline, the five lowest 2022 proxy shares (Searcy, Newton, Cross, Prairie, Montgomery).
  • Non-loss counties: no drop in Democratic Senate-primary votes (52 counties). Raw numbers only.
  • Narrowed-out counties: counties that distort the fit (Chicot, Dallas, Desha, Franklin, Jefferson, Lee, Phillips, Pulaski, Sevier, Washington).
